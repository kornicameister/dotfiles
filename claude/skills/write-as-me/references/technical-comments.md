# User-edited technical comments (sanitized)

Source: English merge-request draft comments edited by the user during an explicit `write-as-me official` task. The original drafts are agent-written; only the user's edits are voice evidence. Of nine comments, five had prose changes, one had a formatting-only change, and three were unchanged. These are excerpts from the actual before/after diff, not newly invented examples.

Sanitized: business names, products, package names, document details, identifiers and links are omitted or replaced with `<A>`, `<B>`, `<endpoint>` and `<subject>`. Do not reuse source-project details or treat the examples as technical evidence.

## Wording question before prescribing changes

Agent: `Please confirm that the intended product is <A> and align the terminology, timeout assumptions and cost model across the document.`

User: `Is that only a verbiage that should be improved and we're actually talking <A>?`

## Ask what was intended; qualify an interpretation

Agent: `Please define one consistent access model: <A>, or <B>.`

User: `It's one or the other I guess. Can we define one consistent access model: <A>, or <B>? Or are both meant to supplement one another?`

This is evidence of tone and an added question about intent, not a rule that the alternatives must be mutually exclusive. Check their technical relationship for each new task.

## Direct question with the explanation retained

Agent: `Please confirm the intended read path and how <subject> receives changes`

User: `Which one is it? Can we confirm the intended read path and how <subject> receives changes`

The user kept the substantive explanation after this change. Four edited comments also changed their opening to lowercase. This permits a conversational opening; it does not require lowercase in every draft.

## Personal judgement in an otherwise technical comment

Agent: `Please clarify whether <endpoint> searches <A> or <B>.`

User: `can you clarify whether <endpoint> searches <A> or <B>.`

Agent: `These are different contracts and <subject>.`

User: `These are different contracts IMHO and <subject>.`

## State the limit of the reading

Agent: `Please clarify the channel rules and migration scope.`

User: `Can you improve/clarify the channel rules and migration scope.`

The user also appended `as far as I can tell` to the observation that the document did not define a behavior.

## Formatting observations and limits

Two comments added selective bold or inline-code emphasis, two put source links on one line, and one changed only identifier formatting. These are sparse observations, not mandatory formatting patterns. Do not copy accidental spaces inside identifiers or escaped/broken backticks.

The recurring evidence supports direct questions, conversational phrasing and occasional qualification of personal interpretation in official inline comments. It does not justify changing the document register, weakening verified facts, adding filler or automatically shortening all comments.
